\section*{Abstract}\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
This experiment addresses desktop-object detection using pencils and tennis balls. It covers dataset organization, unified YOLO-format annotation, YOLO26n training, independent test-set evaluation, Jetson Orin deployment, and ROS 2 publication. Four public sources from Mendeley Data, Kaggle, and Roboflow Universe were merged. Five pencil subclasses were mapped to \texttt{pencil}, and all tennis-ball labels to \texttt{tennis\_ball}. The final dataset contains 895 images and 1,048 objects: 605 training, 171 validation, and 119 test images. The test set was not used for training or best-weight selection.

The model completed a formal 100-epoch augmented run on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 Laptop GPU; the best weights occurred at epoch 87. Validation Precision, Recall, mAP@0.5, and mAP@0.5:0.95 were 0.96338, 0.97329, 0.98185, and 0.88025. Independent-test results were 0.98877, 0.90186, 0.97148, and 0.87761. PT, ONNX, and TensorRT Engine formats were produced. On the Jetson, the camera image topic averaged 13.795 FPS and detection FPS averaged 16.657 FPS.

Only the second on-device test was used for final acceptance. With seed 20260830, it selected 20 pencil and 20 tennis-ball images from the held-out test set. Thirty-nine of 40 were correct: 97.5\% overall, 95.0\% for pencils, and 100.0\% for tennis balls. The only error was a missed red pencil; all 40 results were retained. The code, data, models, video, instructions, and incremental Git history are public. All course requirements were met.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} object detection; YOLO26n; Jetson Orin; TensorRT; ROS 2; pencil; tennis ball

\section{Experiment Requirements and Overall Completion}
\subsection{Experiment Requirements}
The course requires at least two desktop-object classes, data collection or public-data preparation and annotation, model training, real-time Jetson display of classes, boxes, and confidence, and ROS 2 publication. Acceptance requires at least 20 tested objects, accuracy of at least 80\%, Jetson speed of at least 5 FPS, and preserved results and representative errors. Required submissions are the dataset, model, programs, result video, instructions, and LaTeX report.

This project uses \texttt{pencil} and \texttt{tennis\_ball}. Pencils are slender and tennis balls approximately round, providing complementary scales and aspect ratios. Both are easy to obtain for USB-camera and Jetson demonstrations.

\subsection{YOLO26n Model Selection}
YOLO unifies classification and box regression in a real-time single-stage detector. YOLO26 uses an end-to-end design and a one-to-one head. YOLO26n is the smallest scale and has low computation at 640-pixel input, which suits an edge device\cite{yolo26paper,yolo26docs}. The nano model was selected to balance accuracy and stable Jetson speed.
For image size $W,H$ and box corners $(x_1,y_1),(x_2,y_2)$, normalized YOLO coordinates are
\begin{equation}x_c=\frac{x_1+x_2}{2W},\quad y_c=\frac{y_1+y_2}{2H},\quad w=\frac{x_2-x_1}{W},\quad h=\frac{y_2-y_1}{H}.\end{equation}
Each label line contains a class ID and $(x_c,y_c,w,h)$ in $[0,1]$.
\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\begin{equation}P=\frac{TP}{TP+FP},\qquad R=\frac{TP}{TP+FN},\qquad F1=\frac{2PR}{P+R}.\end{equation}
AP is the area under the Precision--Recall curve. mAP@0.5 uses IoU 0.5; mAP@0.5:0.95 averages thresholds from 0.5 to 0.95 in steps of 0.05. Course acceptance accuracy is computed separately from correct item-level outcomes.

\section{Dataset Construction and Annotation Processing}
\subsection{Data Sources and Licensing}
Four public sources were combined. Mendeley Tennis Ball v2 supplied 150 images under CC BY 4.0, after removal of one exact duplicate. Kaggle Pencil supplied 300 images; JSON/COCO boxes were converted to YOLO, but the downloaded page did not specify a license, which remains a documented compliance limitation. Roboflow Pencil v2 supplied 94 images under CC BY 4.0, with five pencil subclasses merged. Roboflow Tennis ball v1 supplied 352 images under Public Domain. Full URLs, licenses, and processing records are in \path{dataset/SOURCES.md} and \path{dataset/dataset_summary.json}\cite{mendeley,kagglepencil,roboflowpencil,roboflowtennis}.

\section{Final 40-Item Acceptance Test and Error Analysis}
\subsection{Test Method}
Two offline attempts were preserved. The first, 10 images per class, was preliminary. Only attempt 2 is final. Seed 20260830 selected 40 unique images only from \path{dataset/images/test}: 20 pencils followed by 20 tennis balls, each with one ground-truth object. The formal TensorRT Engine used confidence 0.25. The highest-confidence detection determined the class; no detection was an error. All evidence is in \path{results/jetson/2026-08-30/acceptance/attempt_02}.
\subsection{Results}
\begin{table}[H]\centering\caption{Second and final Jetson acceptance test}\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}\toprule Class & Tested & Correct & Errors & Accuracy\\\midrule pencil & 20 & 19 & 1 & 95.0\%\\ tennis\_ball & 20 & 20 & 0 & 100.0\%\\\midrule Total & 40 & 39 & 1 & 97.5\%\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{table}
\begin{equation}\mathrm{Accuracy}=\frac{39}{40}\times100\%=97.5\%.\end{equation}
This is 17.5 percentage points above the threshold. Offline TensorRT averaged 31.7906 FPS and reached a minimum of 30.1659 FPS; it excludes camera capture and ROS 2 publication.
\begin{figure}[H]\centering\includegraphics[width=.72\textwidth]{acceptance_correct.png}\caption{Correct tennis-ball acceptance case}\label{fig:acceptcorrect}\end{figure}
\subsection{Sole Error Case}
The only error was a missed \path{pencil_red4.jpg}. No detection exceeded 0.25, so the prediction was empty with confidence zero. It was not removed, replaced, or resampled.
\begin{figure}[H]\centering\includegraphics[width=.78\textwidth]{acceptance_error.png}\caption{Sole error: missed red pencil}\label{fig:accepterror}\end{figure}
The pencil is thin and small, with interference from horizontal background lines. The main risk is assignment to background, not class confusion. Future improvements include more low-contrast slender objects, mixed scenes, threshold calibration, and higher resolution. The test error was not used to retrain and retest, avoiding leakage.

\section{Conclusions and Future Improvements}
The project completed the full path from data to edge deployment: 895 images and 1,048 objects; 100 training epochs with best epoch 87; independent-test mAP@0.5 of 0.97148 and mAP@0.5:0.95 of 0.87761; Jetson camera-topic average of 13.795 FPS; three ROS 2 outputs; and 39/40 final acceptance accuracy of 97.5\%. CSV, JSON, plots, matrices, images, logs, video, and Git history support every result.

Validation, independent testing, and acceptance serve different purposes. TensorRT Engines are environment-dependent, so hashes, software, and hardware must be recorded together. Preserving the sole missed detection and continuous commit history makes the work traceable. Future work should add low-light, occluded, complex, and mixed-class desktop scenes; complement the fixed test with physical camera acceptance; and replace JSON strings with a custom ROS 2 message plus time synchronization and coordinate transforms. A JetPack or Jetson change requires Engine regeneration and repeated speed and accuracy tests.
